## 1. Why enquiries get lost
Most small studios receive questions through several doors at once. Without one place where every enquiry lands, the same person may be answered twice, or not at all. The goal of a workflow is not automation for its own sake; it is making sure every real person gets a timely, appropriate reply from someone who can help.

## 2. The five stages
1. **Capture** — every form has clear labels, asks only what you need, and tells people what happens next. Keep health details out of open text unless a qualified teacher needs them and the person has agreed.
2. **Confirm** — show an on-screen confirmation that is honest ("We've received your enquiry; the team usually replies by …" — *Sonu to supply the real reply window or remove*).
3. **Sort** — give each enquiry a status (New, Contacted, Interested, Trial booked, Converted, Lost, On hold). A status list beats a free-text inbox.
4. **Hand off** — route to the right person: private 1-on-1, group online, studio, corporate/school.
5. **Follow up** — one scheduled follow-up if there is no reply, then stop. Record the outcome.

## 4. WhatsApp boundaries
- Message only people who contacted you first or explicitly asked to be contacted on that channel.
- Do not send bulk promotional messages from a personal number.
- Never ask for or forward medical details by chat; move those to a teacher conversation with consent.
- Keep alert notifications to staff minimal (status, service, first name at most) — not full message text.

## 5. Human hand-off
Automation can sort, remind and summarise. A person should: answer pricing nuances, discuss health concerns (and refer to a medical professional where appropriate), agree schedules, and close the conversation. Make the hand-off visible: "A member of our team will reply."
